# Domain Overview

This document describes the domain and boundaries of the Mirror Presence Layer.

The Mirror Presence Layer models a concrete interaction around a Moment. A Human opens a video Moment and becomes present within it as its author. Other Humans may respond to the Moment and, when their Response is accepted, enter it as participants.

The layer is responsible for the lifecycle of Moments, Responses, and Presences. It does not determine which Moments are shown to a Human or why they are shown. That responsibility belongs to a separate future Resonance layer.

Memory allows a Human’s manifestations to remain in Mirror after the interactions in which they appeared. A Moment’s Gesture and a Response’s Words are recorded in Memory. Over time, these records form a history of how the Human has manifested within Mirror.

The domain includes six core concepts: User, Human, Moment, Response, Presence, and Memory.

## Core Concepts

### User

A User is a technical account that provides access to Mirror and stores account-related information.

### Human

A Human is the domain representation of a person within Mirror.

Unlike a User, a Human does not represent technical access to the system. It is the central entity around which Moments, Responses, Presence records, and Memory records are connected.

### Moment

A Moment is a video space opened by a Human. Its author may use it to share a state, a situation, or a fragment of their life.

Each Moment contains a Gesture — its only written form — which gives the Moment its initial meaning or way of entering it.

### Response

A Response is a Human’s reply to another Human’s Moment.

Its Words express how the Human approaches the Moment. If the author accepts the Response, the responding Human may enter the Moment as a participant.

### Presence

A Presence records a Human’s participation in a specific Moment.

An author Presence is created when the Moment is opened. A participant Presence is created when the author accepts another Human’s Response.

### Memory

A Memory record captures a trace of a Human within a Moment, formed from the Gesture used to open the Moment or the Words submitted in a Response.
